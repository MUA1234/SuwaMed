Chapter 01 – Introduction 

1.1 Background and Context 

In Sri Lanka, the quality of health care varies considerably between urban and rural areas. Rural 
populations lack adequate health care infrastructure, qualified medical professionals, and reliable 
transportation  to  urban  hospitals.  This  creates  a  serious  health  care  gap  where  rural  residents 
experience delayed diagnosis, inadequate treatment, and increased health risks. 

Digital technology and mobile connectivity have created opportunities to democratize health care 
access. While mobile phone penetration extends even to rural areas of Sri Lanka, the healthcare 
sector  has  not  taken  full  advantage  of  this  infrastructure  to  address  the  urban-rural  healthcare 
divide.  Research  indicates  that  properly  designed  telemedicine  systems  can  provide  rural 
populations equitable access to health care resources without the need for physical travel. 

Current health care models rely heavily on physical presence, creating substantial barriers. 
Patients must take time off work, arrange transportation, and incur significant expenses for basic 
consultations. Meanwhile, qualified doctors remain concentrated in urban areas, with their 
expertise underutilized by rural populations. The COVID-19 pandemic further highlighted these 
disparities, demonstrating the urgent need for remote health care solutions. 

1.2 Problem Statement 

When individuals experience clinical symptoms, they often lack immediate access to professional 
medical  advice, leading  to  unreliable self-diagnosis or delayed consultation. The cost  and time 
required  to  travel  to  urban  health  facilities  pose  considerable  barriers,  particularly  for  elderly 
patients, people with mobility problems, or families with limited resources. 

While telemedicine has emerged globally, existing platforms fail to meet the specific needs of 
Sri Lankan rural communities. Many international telehealth applications require high-speed 
Internet, require digital literacy, and lack local language support. These platforms rarely integrate 
culturally appropriate interfaces or account for the unique health care ecosystem in Sri Lanka, 
including local payment systems, endemic diseases such as dengue, and doctor-patient 
communication styles.

Chapter 02 – Business Case 

2.1 Business Need 

The lack of a comprehensive, locally focused telehealth platform means that rural communities 
in Sri Lanka suffer from preventable health complications and limited access to timely medical 
intervention. The project addresses the critical health care access challenges faced by millions of 
Sri Lankans. 

Key Business Drivers: 

•  Rural populations face 2-4 hour travel times for basic medical consultations 

•  Average consultation cost including travel expenses exceeds LKR 2,000 per visit 

•  Existing international platforms (oDoc, Practo, WebMD) do not address local language 

needs or low-bandwidth environments 

•  Over 65% of Sri Lanka’s population resides outside major urban centers with limited 

healthcare access 

•  Post-pandemic awareness has increased demand for remote healthcare solutions

through remote access 

2.  Achieve platform accessibility for users on 2G/3G networks while maintaining video

patient reach by 300% 

4.  Establish a sustainable subscription-based revenue model with tiered pricing for different 

user segments 

5.  Integrate local payment gateways (LankaQR, iPay, card payments) achieving 95% 

transaction success rate 

6.  Build user trust through verified doctor credentials and patient rating system achieving 

minimum 4.0 average rating

3.1 Functional Deliverables 

Objective 1: Develop a Cross-Platform Telehealth Application 

Build a mobile application for iOS and Android using Flutter, and a web application using React 
with a Firebase backend for real-time data synchronization, authentication, and cloud storage. 
The system will support responsive design across all device types. 

Objective 2: Implement AI-Powered Symptom Checker 

Develop  an  intelligent  symptom  assessment  system  with  structured  questionnaires  in  Sinhala, 
Tamil  and  English.  The  system  will  provide  initial  health  assessments  and  suggest  when 
professional  consultation  is  needed,  trained  on  Sri  Lankan  disease  patterns  including  endemic 
conditions like dengue. 

Objective 3: Integrate Secure Video and Chat Consultation 

Implement end-to-end encrypted video conferencing and messaging using WebRTC/Agora.io, 
optimized for low-bandwidth connections (2G/3G). Enable real-time doctor-patient consultation 
with digital prescription capabilities. 

Objective 4: Create Comprehensive Appointment Management 

Develop a two-way platform that allows patients to find and book consultations based on 
expertise, language, ratings and fees, while enabling doctors to manage calendars, schedule 
availability and set consultation fees.

Implement AES-256 encrypted health record storage with role-based access controls. Patients 
can maintain health history and upload documents, while doctors can access relevant information 
during consultation.

Integrate  local  payment  gateways  (LankaQR,  iPay,  card  payments)  supporting  different 
subscription tiers with automated billing. The system will include income dashboards for doctors 
with payment tracking.

Chapter 04 – Literature Review 

4.1 Existing Solutions Analysis 

oDoc (Sri Lanka) 

Sri Lanka’s leading telehealth platform offers video consultations and home healthcare. However, 
it  primarily  targets  urban  populations  with  reliable  internet  access.  It  lacks  an  integrated  AI 
symptom checker, has limited language support, and requires high bandwidth. Its cost structure 
may be prohibitive for rural populations. 

WebMD Symptom Checker 

Provides comprehensive symptom screening but  is designed for Western healthcare  systems.  It 
lacks localization for Sri Lankan endemic diseases, has no local language support, and does not 
connect users to local doctors or integrate with local healthcare providers. 

Practo (India) 

Offers comprehensive healthcare services but faces integration challenges with Sri Lankan medical 
systems, payment gateways, and regulations. It is optimized for the Indian urban environment and 
does not address rural bandwidth challenges specific to Sri Lanka. 

Doctor on Call (Sri Lanka) 

Provides telephone consultation but lacks video capabilities, symptom assessment tools, and 
digital health record management, limiting its effectiveness for comprehensive health care 
delivery. 

4.2 Identified Research Gaps 

Knowledge Gap 

Limited research exists on integrated telehealth platforms combining AI symptom assessment, 
video consultation and health records specifically for Sri Lankan rural communities. Most 
research focuses on components independently rather than integrated, culturally appropriate 
systems. 

Technological Gap 

Current solutions do not adequately address Sri Lankan rural technological challenges: 
intermittent electricity, low bandwidth connections (2G/3G), and limited smartphone 
capabilities. There is a significant gap in developing applications that provide quality 
consultation over 2G while maintaining security and user experience. 

Methodological Gap 

Existing symptom checkers use rule-based algorithms from Western medical databases without 
machine learning adapted to local disease patterns and spatial conditions. Current platforms lack

mechanisms for continuous improvement through local medical data and do not account for Sri 
Lankan doctor-patient communication styles. 

Contextual Gap 

International platforms are not designed with Sri Lankan regulatory requirements, payment 
systems or cultural expectations in mind. They do not integrate local payment gateways, lack 
Sinhala/Tamil support, and do not focus on the trust-building needed in communities where face-
to-face health care has been the norm. 

4.3 How SuwaMed Addresses These Gaps 

SuwaMed provides a local-centric ecosystem addressing the identified gaps. Unlike oDoc’s urban 
approach,  SuwaMed  optimizes  for  low-bandwidth  through  adaptive  video  quality  and  2G/3G 
compatibility. It offers full multilingual support with culturally appropriate medical terminology 
in Sinhala, Tamil, and English. 

Unlike  WebMD,  SuvaMed's  AI  will  train  on  Sri  Lankan  disease  patterns,  including  endemic 
conditions like dengue. The Symptom Checker connects seamlessly to the doctor's consultation, 
creating an integrated health care journey rather than separate assessments. 

Key  innovations  include:  integrated  ecosystem  combining  symptom  screening,  counseling  and 
health  records;  Dual  platform  access  (mobile  and  web);  Affordable  subscription  model;  low-
bandwidth optimization; encrypted health records; and local payment integration. For doctors, it 
enables  practice  expansion  beyond  geographical  boundaries  with  flexible  scheduling,  patient 
management, and earnings analysis.
